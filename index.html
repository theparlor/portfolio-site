An operating model, measured

One operator. Eighty-five repositories.
One way of running them.

One person runs 85 repositories under one versioned session kit, now at version 42 on every one of them. Every agent session works in its own copy of a repository and lands its work straight to main on plain git: 541 landings in one measured week, about 77 a day, with 3.6 percent of land attempts failed or blocked. The system measures its own use from an append-only event log, and the rules it runs on are numbered decisions, 176 of them. The engines below are the evidence for that operating model. They are not products to install: every engine repository is private and assumes this one folder tree.

What the operating model built

Twenty-eight repositories, each with its own git history and decision log: counting. The labels are not opinion. The system sorted its own engines from 60 days of use events, git and the session ledger, and labelled two of them parked. They fall into two tiers: Products aimed at work beyond the system itself, and The Foundry that makes and runs them. Seven still carry a working name that describes a mechanism instead of a purpose. Each of those is shown here by what it is for, with the working name beneath, and where one holds many smaller tasks the card lists them. Behind the veil

None of these came from nowhere.

Every implement above was forged by the same machinery. Two systems guided their development: one is the operating model that built them, the other is the overhead view that holds them together. This is where the warm metalwork cools into the cold architecture underneath.

The operating model

Intent

How each product is actually built: a continuous loop of Notice, Spec, Execute and Observe, replacing ceremony with artifacts that compound. Most products here keep their own decision log, traced in git, and the workspace keeps 176 numbered decision records above them.
